Zero to alive

The path to life

Six steps take Ember from an empty grid of numbers to a thing that knows its own name and loads into your kernel. Only one of them needs your actual graphics card.

1

Gather the words

Stream ~7 billion tokens of real, cleaned web text (FineWeb-Edu). No download of the whole internet — it stops when it has enough.

2

Pretrain — learn language

the weekend

~54 hours on one RTX 5080, with the Muon optimizer. Ember learns to write. This is the long one, and the one step that needs the real card.

3

Fine-tune — learn to talk

Minutes, not days. Shown example conversations, Ember learns to answer — and learns that it is Ember.

4

Readiness — the milestone

proven · 6/6

Asked who it is, in wordings it was never trained on. It names itself every time. That's the thing you asked for: it can tell you what it is.

5

Chat

A normal back-and-forth. Talk to it about Astrion, ask it simple things.

6

Export into the kernel

proven · 1.0000

Shrunk to an 8-bit ember.astrion file (~340 MB) your OS loads. Same engine as Qwen — just a different file.

Straight with you

What's real, and what waits

Everything marked proven above really ran — on a small stand-in model, here on the Mac, so the wiring is honest end-to-end.

Ember's actual language and smarts come from the weekend pretrain on your 5080 — the one step no laptop can fake. Until then the little demo knows its name but can't hold a real conversation.

Identity always comes from the fine-tune examples, never from the model "waking up." That's true of every assistant, ChatGPT included. It's not a trick — it's how they learn who they are.
