# Astrion — Competitive Brief (July 2026, updated 2026-09-13)

Honest read of the AI-OS landscape and where Astrion actually fits. No hype —
the point is to know the truth so you position and pitch it right.

**Sept 13 update, read this first:** the "kid + Claude + from-scratch OS" story
is no longer ours alone. VibeOS (Kaan Şenol, a student at Politecnico di Torino)
shipped a from-scratch aarch64 hobby OS "vibecoded completely from scratch with
Claude Code" in March 2026 and has 1.5k stars, a Wikipedia page, Hackaday and
Futurism coverage, and a running Doom. Microsoft then put a *different*
"VibeOS" on the Build 2026 stage. The story axis is crowded. The axis that is
still empty is the one VibeOS does not touch: **the model runs inside the
kernel.** VibeOS was written *by* an AI; it does not *run* one. Details in the
VibeOS section below, and the positioning section is rewritten around it.

---

## TL;DR

- **2026 is the year on-device AI went mainstream.** That's a huge *tailwind*
  for Astrion's "local-first AI" thesis — and a *threat*, because the giants
  now all do it too.
- **Every serious "AI OS" bolts AI onto an existing OS** (Windows, macOS,
  Linux). Microsoft, Apple, and the agent-OS startups are all wrappers +
  models on top of a decades-old kernel.
- **The famous from-scratch OSes have no AI in them.** SerenityOS has none.
  VibeOS was generated by Claude but runs no model.
- So **nobody occupies Astrion's exact square: a from-scratch kernel with a
  language model running inside it, offline, with a safety story.** That
  intersection is real and unclaimed — but it's a *story/vision/education*
  edge, **not** an AI-capability edge. Astrion's tiny model can't out-answer
  Apple's 3B or Microsoft's 14B in-box models, and shouldn't pretend to.

---

## The 2026 market shift (context)

On-device AI crossed into the mainstream this year: Ollama passed ~52M monthly
downloads, Meta's ExecuTorch hit 1.0, and flagship chips (Apple M4/A18 ~38
TOPS, Snapdragon X Elite ~45 TOPS) made local 1–5 GB models "GPT-3.5-class."
Latency + privacy are the drivers (cloud adds 200–500 ms; on-device is <20 ms/
token). Translation: **"local AI" is no longer a differentiator by itself —
it's becoming table stakes.** Astrion needs an edge *beyond* "it's local."

### VibeOS, in detail (added 2026-09-13)

What it is, verified against the repo and three write-ups:

- `github.com/kaansenol5/VibeOS` — "A completely vibecoded operating system
  targeting Aarch64." The repo's own CLAUDE.md: "vibecoded completely from
  scratch with Claude Code." 64 session logs are public. Creator: Kaan Şenol,
  Politecnico di Torino. Covered by Hackaday (2026-03-04), MyBroadband,
  Futurism ("Entirely Vibe-Coded Operating System Is a Bug-Filled Disaster"),
  and it has a Wikipedia article.
- Runs on QEMU virt and a Raspberry Pi Zero 2W. Custom kernel, cooperative
  multitasking, FAT32, memory allocator, virtio-net + Ethernet/ARP/IP/ICMP/UDP/
  DNS/TCP/HTTP/TLS 1.2, a browser with HTML/CSS rendering, a vim clone, MP3
  player, Doom, Tiny C Compiler, MicroPython. MIT (core).
- Its own README: "Not everything works, some stuff is not even tested."

What it takes from us: the headline "one young person + Claude wrote an OS from
scratch." That is now a March 2026 story with 1.5k stars. Leading with it in
September reads as late.

What it does not have, and cannot claim without becoming a different project:

1. **No model runs inside it.** VibeOS's Claude is on Anthropic's servers at
   development time. Astrion's transformer runtime is in the kernel and the
   Assistant answers from kernel data with no path to the network. That is the
   axis.
2. **No hardware protection story.** Cooperative multitasking, no ring-3
   isolation with per-process address spaces. Astrion's `exec rogue.elf` beat
   (the CPU kills a hostile program) has no VibeOS equivalent.
3. **It runs on real hardware and we do not.** Pi Zero 2W boots are a real
   credential we lack. Do not pitch against VibeOS on hardware.
4. **It has a browser and a TCP stack we do not.** Do not pitch against it on
   networking either. Our `net` command is ARP/DHCP/ping/DNS on a virtual card.

## Where Astrion is genuinely differentiated

The honest, defensible claims:

1. **The model runs inside the kernel.** SerenityOS is from-scratch with no AI.
   VibeOS is from-scratch and was *written by* an AI. Microsoft/Apple have AI
   on old kernels. Astrion is the only one with a language-model runtime
   compiled into a from-scratch kernel, fed by a boot module, running the
   forward pass on the CPU with nothing underneath. That is unclaimed.
2. **The AI has no path to the network.** The kernel now has a NIC driver and
   a small stack for the Terminal's `net` command, so "no network stack" is no
   longer the claim. The claim is narrower and still true: the Assistant and
   the model runtime live inside the kernel and nothing routes them to the
   network. That is a stronger privacy story than "on-device but hybrid-cloud"
   (Apple/MS both fall back to cloud) — and unlike theirs, a person can read
   the whole kernel and check it.
3. **The safety story.** Ring-3 isolation with per-process page tables and a
   syscall boundary means untrusted programs are contained at the CPU level —
   a kernel-level safety posture the agent-OS startups (which run on someone
   else's kernel) structurally can't match, and VibeOS's cooperative kernel
   does not attempt.
4. **The founder story, told precisely.** A 12-year-old directing Claude to
   build a from-scratch OS *with an AI in it*, and verifying every build on a
   real boot. "Kid + Claude + OS" alone is VibeOS's headline now; "the model is
   inside the kernel" is what makes ours different, so the story must always
   be told with that clause attached.

## Where Astrion is NOT competitive (be honest)

- **AI capability:** a ~212K-param char model vs Apple's 3B / Microsoft's 14B.
  Not close, and it shouldn't claim to be. Astrion's AI is a *proof it runs at
  all on a from-scratch OS*, not a useful assistant yet. And the released ISO
  currently ships without the brain module at all.
- **Ecosystem, apps, drivers, hardware support:** effectively zero vs decades.
- **Real-hardware readiness:** never booted on metal. VibeOS has.
- **Networking:** VibeOS has TCP/TLS/HTTP and a browser; we have ARP/DHCP/
  ping/DNS in a Terminal command.

## Positioning recommendation (rewritten 2026-09-13)

Don't pitch Astrion as "a better AI assistant than Apple/Microsoft" — you'll
lose that framing instantly. Don't lead with "a kid and Claude built an OS" —
VibeOS owns that headline since March. Pitch the **thing only we have**:

> "The big companies add AI to 40-year-old operating systems. VibeOS used an
> AI to write one. I'm building the one where the model runs *inside* the
> kernel — a transformer doing its math in a kernel I wrote from scratch, with
> no path from it to the network."

Lead with: **the model runs inside the kernel + the AI has no path to the
network + safe by the CPU + a 12-year-old directs it and boots every build.**

**Best near-term wedge:** education / "understandable AI-native OS" (the
SerenityOS "built to be understood" playbook, but AI-native) and the
privacy-purist niche ("an AI you can read all the way down, that cannot reach
the network").

## Threats & tailwinds

- **Tailwind:** "local AI" is now credible and desirable to normal people —
  the market believes the thesis. Astrion is early to *AI inside a from-scratch
  kernel*.
- **Threat:** "local AI" is commoditizing, so Astrion must own the *model-in-
  the-kernel + safety* angle, not the "it's local" angle (which Apple/MS now
  also claim).
- **Threat:** the "AI-built OS" headline is VibeOS's. Every month we lead with
  the founder story instead of the artifact, we look like the second one.
- **Watch:** the agentic-OS startups — if one ships a genuinely safe local
  agent runtime with real traction, they own the "safe local AI" narrative on
  top of Linux. Astrion's counter is *kernel-level* safety they can't match.
- **Watch:** VibeOS adding an on-device model. It has the hardware and the
  TCP stack; a llama.cpp port would take our axis. Ship the brain module in
  the ISO before that happens.
